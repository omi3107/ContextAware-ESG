# ESG-Lens: Context-Aware ESG Comparability

## Overview

ESG-Lens is a research project focused on determining whether Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) figures can be fairly and reliably compared across companies, reporting frameworks, and time periods.

ESG figures may appear similar but represent different concepts, definitions, reporting boundaries, measurement methods, or time periods. Comparing such figures without considering their underlying context can lead to misleading conclusions.

This research aims to address this challenge by evaluating the meaning and context behind ESG figures before determining whether they are genuinely comparable.

## Research Objectives

- Assess the comparability of ESG figures across different companies.
- Identify differences in metric definitions, units, boundaries, and measurement methods.
- Examine consistency in ESG disclosures across different reporting frameworks.
- Track changes in ESG figures and disclosures over multiple years.
- Identify missing, inconsistent, or redefined disclosures.
- Explain why a comparison is valid, invalid, or uncertain.

## Comparison Scenarios

The research considers four primary comparison scenarios:

1. **Different Companies – Different Industries:** Comparing ESG figures between companies operating in different industries.
2. **Different Companies – Same Industry:** Comparing ESG figures between companies operating in the same industry.
3. **Same Company – Different Frameworks:** Comparing disclosures under frameworks such as GRI, ISSB, ESRS, and BRSR.
4. **Same Company – Different Years:** Examining changes in ESG figures and disclosures over time.

## Core Research Principle

**Don't compare numbers first. Compare what the numbers mean first.**

A numerical comparison should only be considered reliable when the underlying concepts and reporting contexts are sufficiently aligned.

Missing or unavailable information should remain unknown rather than being treated as positive or negative performance.

## Current Research Scope

The current empirical implementation focuses on environmental indicators, including:

- Greenhouse Gas (GHG) emissions
- Energy consumption
- Water consumption and withdrawal
- Waste generation and management

Social and Governance dimensions are acknowledged in the broader research context but are outside the current empirical evaluation scope.

## Expected Outcome

The intended outcome is a system that can assess ESG figure comparability and provide:

- A comparability verdict.
- Reasons supporting the verdict.
- Identification of important contextual differences.
- Evidence supporting the assessment.
- Disclosure gaps and historical inconsistencies.
- An explanation of uncertainty where sufficient evidence is unavailable.

## Research Vision

The broader vision is to make ESG comparisons more transparent, meaningful, and reliable by ensuring that figures are evaluated based on their actual context rather than numerical similarity alone.
